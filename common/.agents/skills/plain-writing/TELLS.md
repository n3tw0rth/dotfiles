# AI tells

Patterns readers link to machine-written text. Find each one and rewrite the sentence it sits in.

## Punctuation and formatting

- Em dash or en dash anywhere. A spaced hyphen or double hyphen used as a dash.
- Colon subtitles on titles ("Kafka: A Complete Guide to Streaming").
- Emoji in headings or bullets.
- Bold lead-in on every bullet ("**Speed:** it is fast").
- Title Case On Every Heading.
- Bold scattered through prose for emphasis.

## Words

delve, leverage, utilize, robust, seamless, comprehensive, crucial, pivotal, vital, paramount, streamline, empower, unlock, elevate, enhance, foster, harness, navigate (figurative), landscape, realm, tapestry, journey, testament, underscore, showcase, intricate, nuanced, holistic, synergy, cutting-edge, game-changer, ever-evolving, dynamic, vibrant, meticulous, effortless, embark, myriad, plethora, bustling, boasts.

## Phrases

- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In the ever-evolving landscape of", "Let's dive in", "Welcome to", "This guide will walk you through", "Have you ever wondered".
- Filler: "It's worth noting", "It's important to note", "Simply put", "At its core", "When it comes to", "plays a crucial role", "a wide range of", "This ensures that", "In order to".
- Sentence starters: Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, Ultimately, Notably, Importantly.
- Hedges: "can potentially", "may help to", "generally speaking", "arguably".
- Closers: "In conclusion", "In summary", "By following these steps, you can", "Happy coding!", "I hope this helps", "Feel free to reach out".

## Structures

- Contrast framing: "It's not X, it's Y." / "Not just X, but Y."
- Reflex triplets: three adjectives or three parallel clauses ("fast, scalable, and reliable").
- Rhetorical question as a transition ("So what does this mean?", "The result?").
- An intro that announces what the doc covers, or an outro that restates it.
- Uniform shape: every section the same length, every bullet the same length, every list exactly 3 or 5 items.
- "Whether you're a beginner or an expert..."
- Sweeping range openers ("From startups to enterprises, ...").
- Claims about significance in place of facts ("This marks a significant step forward").
- Signposting chains (First... Next... Then... Finally...) in prose that isn't a procedure.
- Vague attribution ("Experts say", "Many teams find").
